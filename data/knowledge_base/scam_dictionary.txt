FINANCIAL SCAMS DICTIONARY — RuralFinance AI Knowledge Base

INTRODUCTION
Financial fraud is a growing problem in India, especially targeting rural communities. In 2023, Indians lost over ₹7,000 crore to cyber fraud. Knowledge is your best protection. Learn to recognize these scams before they find you.

TOP FINANCIAL SCAMS

1. OTP FRAUD (One-Time Password Scam)
How It Works:
- You receive a call claiming to be from your bank, UIDAI, or telecom company.
- The caller says your account/SIM/Aadhaar will be blocked unless you verify.
- They ask you to share the OTP sent to your phone.
- Once they have the OTP, they drain your bank account within minutes.
Red Flags:
- Any call asking for OTP is a scam — banks NEVER ask for OTP.
- Caller creates urgency: "You have 2 hours before your account is blocked."
- Unusual caller ID from a mobile number (not a registered bank number).
What To Do: Hang up immediately. Block the number. Check your account. Call your bank's official number.

2. FAKE LOAN APP FRAUD
How It Works:
- Apps on Google Play or social media promise instant personal loans without documents.
- They ask permission to access your contacts, gallery, and messages.
- After "loan approval," they demand processing fees upfront (₹500 to ₹5,000).
- They may also collect your private photos and blackmail you.
Red Flags:
- No physical office address, no RBI registration number.
- Demands upfront fee before releasing loan — legitimate banks never do this.
- Requests permission to access all your phone content.
- Promises loans regardless of credit score or income.
What To Do: Check RBI's registered NBFC list at rbi.org.in before using any loan app.

3. KYC UPDATE FRAUD
How It Works:
- You receive an SMS or call saying your KYC is expired and account will freeze.
- You are asked to click a link, download an app, or share Aadhaar/PAN details.
- The link installs remote-access software (like AnyDesk) on your phone.
- They can now see everything on your screen, including banking apps.
Red Flags:
- Bank SMS asking you to click a link for KYC update.
- Calls asking for Aadhaar or PAN number.
- Requests to install "verification" apps.
What To Do: KYC update can ONLY be done by visiting your bank branch or via official bank app/website. Ignore any links.

4. LOTTERY AND PRIZE SCAM
How It Works:
- You receive a message: "Congratulations! You've won ₹25 lakh in KBC/Jio/Amazon lottery!"
- To claim your prize, you must pay a small "processing fee," "GST," or "tax" upfront.
- Once paid, they ask for more fees. There is never a prize.
Red Flags:
- You never participated in any lottery.
- Must pay to receive prize — legitimate prizes never have upfront fees.
- Creates urgency: "Claim within 24 hours."
- Poor grammar and spelling in messages.
What To Do: Ignore and delete. Report to cybercrime.gov.in.

5. INVESTMENT PONZI SCHEME
How It Works:
- Promises extraordinary returns: "30% monthly returns guaranteed!"
- Early investors receive payment from new investor money (not actual profits).
- Eventually collapses when new investors stop joining, leaving most with nothing.
- Often promoted by WhatsApp, Telegram groups, or "trusted" community members.
Red Flags:
- Returns above 12-15% per year should raise serious questions.
- "Guaranteed returns" — legitimate investments carry risk.
- Pressure to recruit friends and family to invest.
- No SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India) registration.
- Vague explanation of "where" the money is invested.
What To Do: Verify any investment company at sebi.gov.in. Never invest based on verbal promises.

6. GOVERNMENT SCHEME IMPERSONATION
How It Works:
- Caller claims to be from PM-KISAN, MGNREGA, Ujjwala Yojana, Ayushman Bharat, or income tax.
- Says you are entitled to benefits but need to "update your details."
- Collects bank account number, Aadhaar, OTP, or makes you download an app.
Red Flags:
- Government schemes NEVER call to collect OTP or bank account details.
- Government never contacts via WhatsApp for official matters.
- Suspicious links claiming to be government websites.
What To Do: Verify by calling the official scheme helpline or visiting your nearest bank/CSC center.

7. FAKE JOB OFFER SCAM
How It Works:
- Job offer via WhatsApp or SMS: "Work from home, earn ₹50,000/month, no qualification needed."
- After "selection," you must pay registration fee, training fee, or uniform fee.
- Money taken, job never materializes.
Red Flags:
- Job offered without any interview process.
- Upfront payment required before starting work.
- Unrealistic salary promise.
- Company name sounds similar to but not the same as a known company.
What To Do: Verify the company on the Ministry of Corporate Affairs website (mca.gov.in). Never pay for a job.

8. SIM SWAP FRAUD
How It Works:
- Fraudster collects your personal details (from social media or earlier fraud).
- They contact your telecom operator and get a new SIM issued for your number.
- Your SIM stops working. They receive all OTPs and bank alerts on the new SIM.
Red Flags:
- Your mobile suddenly loses network for no reason.
- Unable to make calls or receive messages.
What To Do: Immediately call your telecom operator. Report to bank to block transactions. File complaint at cybercrime.gov.in.

9. PHISHING EMAIL/SMS FRAUD
How It Works:
- You receive an email or SMS looking exactly like your bank's communication.
- The link takes you to a fake website that looks like your bank's website.
- You enter login ID, password, and OTP — all captured by fraudsters.
Red Flags:
- Links containing misspellings: "sbi.co.fraud.com" or "hdfc-secure.info"
- Email from Gmail/Yahoo/Outlook addresses instead of official bank domain.
- Asks for confidential details that banks never ask for online.
What To Do: Always type your bank's URL directly. Never click links in SMS/emails for banking.

10. FAKE INSURANCE POLICY FRAUD
How It Works:
- Caller offers very cheap life or health insurance with huge coverage.
- Asks for premium payment upfront via UPI or bank transfer.
- Policy documents are fake. Company doesn't exist.
Red Flags:
- Unsolicited call offering insurance.
- Premium far lower than market rates.
- Insists on immediate payment.
What To Do: Buy insurance ONLY from IRDAI-registered companies. Verify at irdai.gov.in.

DANGEROUS LOAN DOCUMENT CLAUSES

Watch for these terms in any loan agreement:
1. BALLOON PAYMENT: A large final payment much bigger than regular EMIs — a debt trap.
2. PREPAYMENT PENALTY: Charging you for paying loan early. Illegal for floating rate home loans.
3. CROSS-DEFAULT CLAUSE: If you default on one loan, all loans become due immediately.
4. AUTOMATIC ROLLOVER: Loan renews automatically with new fees if not closed in time.
5. FLOATING RATE WITHOUT CAP: Interest rate can rise without any upper limit.
6. PERSONAL GUARANTEE: Your personal assets liable even if you took a business loan.
7. ARBITRATION CLAUSE: Forces disputes to private arbitration instead of consumer courts.

HIGH-COST LOAN RED FLAGS:
- Annual interest rate above 24% is very high (personal loan limit should be 18-22%).
- Processing fee above 2% of loan amount is excessive.
- Total "other charges" are not clearly listed in the agreement.
- Being forced to buy insurance as condition for loan approval.

HOW TO REPORT FINANCIAL FRAUD

Immediate Steps (Act within 24 hours for best chance of recovery):
1. Call your bank's fraud helpline immediately to block your account/card.
2. File complaint online at: cybercrime.gov.in (National Cyber Crime Portal).
3. Call Cyber Crime Helpline: 1930 (available 24/7).
4. Visit your nearest police station for FIR (for amounts above ₹10,000).
5. File complaint with RBI Banking Ombudsman for bank-related fraud.

Important Documents to Collect:
- Screenshot of all suspicious messages and calls.
- Transaction reference numbers from your bank statement.
- Any fake documents or agreements received.
- Call recordings if possible.

Official Verification Resources:
- RBI Registered NBFCs: rbi.org.in → NBFC List
- SEBI Registered Investment Advisors: sebi.gov.in → Registered Entities
- IRDAI Registered Insurers: irdai.gov.in → Registered Entities
- Verify Aadhaar: myaadhaar.uidai.gov.in
